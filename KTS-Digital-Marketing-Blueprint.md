# KTS Digital Marketing — company blueprint

Working launch plan · 6 September 2026

## 1. Venture identity

**Name:** KTS Digital Marketing  
**Positioning:** A full-service agency connecting brand strategy, creative execution and digital performance for growing businesses.  
**Brand line:** Good brands. Bold moves. Real growth.  
**Relationship:** A dedicated business venture within the KTS family, with its own website, proposals, client pipeline, delivery ownership and financial tracking.

This is a proposed operating structure. It does not represent company registration, an established team, existing clients or confirmed financial results. The founder must decide the legal entity, ownership, dedicated contact details and final commercial terms before launch.

## 2. Initial customers and focus

Offer the full service range, but concentrate early outreach on two segments: local service businesses and education/training businesses. These are proposed starting segments, based on the existing KTS network, not verified market research. Expand into retail and e-commerce once delivery capacity and relevant evidence exist.

The initial customer needs a clearer brand, a credible website and a repeatable way to attract enquiries. Qualify each opportunity for its offer, decision maker, available budget, sales follow-up capacity and realistic timeline.

## 3. Service portfolio

| Practice | Core deliverables | Measures agreed with each client |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand and strategy | Discovery, positioning, identity, messaging, channel roadmap | Approved brand system and prioritised plan |
| Social and content | Content calendars, copy, design, reels, video, community support | Relevant engagement, qualified traffic and enquiries |
| SEO and local discovery | Technical audit, on-page work, content, business profile support | Search visibility, organic conversions, local enquiries |
| Paid media | Search/social campaigns, audiences, creative tests, remarketing | Qualified lead cost, conversion rate, attributable revenue where measurable |
| Web and commerce | Business sites, landing pages, storefronts, conversion improvements | Usability, enquiry/purchase conversion and site performance |
| Retention and insights | Email, consent-based messaging, CRM journeys, analytics, reporting | Repeat conversions, journey completion and reporting quality |
| Specialist projects | Influencer coordination, reputation support, campaign production | Scope-specific milestones and attributable outcomes |

For each proposal specify quantities, channels, review rounds, client inputs, turnaround times, exclusions and success measures. Avoid selling unlimited work.

## 4. Team and accountability

```text
Founder / Venture Lead
├── Business Development & Client Strategy
│   └── Discovery, proposals, account ownership, renewals
├── Creative & Content
│   └── Copy, design, social, video, brand consistency
├── Performance & Search
│   └── Paid media, SEO, tracking, reporting
├── Web & Automation
│   └── Websites, landing pages, CRM and email journeys
└── Operations & Finance
    └── Capacity, contracts, invoicing and quality checks
```

Start with roles rather than five departments. The founder can own sales and account strategy; a core creative generalist and a performance/SEO specialist can cover recurring delivery. Use scoped specialists for development, video and advanced campaigns. Assign one accountable owner to every client, even when several people contribute.

Add capacity only when signed work can sustain it and workloads show a repeated constraint. Track available hours, committed hours and actual delivery time weekly.

## 5. Commercial model

- **Launch:** Fixed-scope projects for identity, website, channel setup and launch planning. Bill against agreed milestones.
- **Grow:** Monthly retainers for a defined mix of content, social, search and campaigns. Agree reporting and review cadence.
- **Optimise:** Paid audits and scoped improvement sprints, with ongoing work proposed only when justified.

Prepare prices from delivery effort rather than inventing public packages. Proposed calculation: `(estimated hours × internal hourly cost + direct supplier costs) ÷ (1 − target gross margin)`. Choose the margin and hourly cost from actual operating costs. Record sales/administration overhead separately when assessing profitability.

Keep agency fees separate from client advertising spend, subscriptions, travel and external production. Wherever possible, clients own their advertising accounts and pay media platforms directly. Agree deposits, payment dates, revision limits, cancellation terms and change requests in the contract.

## 6. Lead-to-delivery workflow

1. **Enquiry:** Log source, business, contact, requested services and next action in a shared pipeline.
2. **Discovery:** Confirm business goals, baseline, budget, decision maker and follow-up process.
3. **Proposal:** State deliverables, exclusions, schedule, fee, success measures and client responsibilities.
4. **Agreement:** Confirm scope, payment schedule, permissions and named owners before work starts.
5. **Onboarding:** Gather assets and role-based account access; document measurement and approval steps.
6. **Planning:** Prepare the first work cycle, prioritise tasks and obtain client approval.
7. **Production and QA:** Check content, brand consistency, links, tracking and relevant consent before launch.
8. **Delivery:** Launch approved work and monitor for technical or campaign problems.
9. **Reporting:** Explain results, limitations, lessons and the next actions; distinguish platform attribution from confirmed sales.
10. **Renewal or handover:** Agree the next scope or transfer client-owned assets and revoke unnecessary access.

Use one pipeline, one project board, a shared asset library, a password manager and an invoicing record. Keep each client's assets and permissions separate. Select products after assessing actual team needs and budget.

## 7. First 90 days

| Period | Priority | Concrete completion criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–15 | Establish the venture | Founder confirms structure, contact, service ownership; proposal, discovery and contract templates ready |
| Days 16–30 | Become launch-ready | Website approved; enquiry route tested; sample creative labelled as concepts; onboarding and QA checklist ready |
| Days 31–60 | Validate delivery | Proposed activity target: 30 qualified outreach conversations, 8 discoveries, 3 proposals; record actual conversion and objections |
| Days 61–90 | Improve and document | Complete agreed pilot work; review costs and capacity; seek permission for real case studies; refine scope and pricing |

These activity targets are planning hypotheses, not forecasts or guarantees. Review them weekly against capacity and response quality. Use existing KTS relationships with permission, founder-led outreach, helpful audits and referral partnerships. Do not manufacture testimonials or results.

## 8. Weekly management view

Track new qualified leads, discoveries, proposals, signed value, collected cash, outstanding invoices, recurring revenue, actual hours, delivery margin, overdue tasks and client retention. For client campaigns, track the few business outcomes agreed in their scope rather than follower counts alone.

Weekly: review pipeline and delivery capacity. Monthly: review client results, profitability and cash needs. Quarterly: reconsider service mix and whether new hiring is justified.
