# KTS Digital Marketing: 90-day working revenue plan

## Target and working assumptions

Collect approximately **₹2,00,000 in agency fees within 90 days**. This is a sales target, not a promise. Count cleared client payments, excluding taxes collected and client advertising budgets. Track delivery obligations against advances; cash collected is not automatically earned accounting revenue or profit.

Day 1 is the day the founder starts this plan. Working assumptions: no current clients, one founder, 5–6 working hours per day for six days a week, existing computer/internet, and no paid acquisition. Availability and budget have not yet been confirmed. If less time is available, reduce delivery scope or extend the target period.

## 1. Sell two focused offers

These are proposed launch prices to test in discovery calls, not researched market averages. The website can present the broader agency range, but actively sell only services the founder can deliver reliably.

| Offer | Proposed fee | Included scope | Boundaries |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Social Presence | ₹15,000 per 30-day cycle, paid before the cycle | 8 static/carousel posts; 4 simple short videos edited from client-supplied footage; captions; scheduling to Instagram and Facebook using adapted versions of the same content; monthly calendar and report | Maximum 5 slides per carousel, 30 seconds per video, 2 consolidated revision rounds per monthly batch. No shooting, daily community management, paid ads, influencer fees or guaranteed leads. |
| Business Website | ₹25,000 fixed project | Up to 5 informational pages; responsive layout; contact/WhatsApp links; page titles/descriptions; client-supplied branding and images; basic copy preparation; 2 consolidated revision rounds | No e-commerce, custom backend, membership or payment integration. Client pays domain/hosting directly. 14-day bug-fix window after handover; ongoing changes quoted separately. |

Website terms: 60% advance to book, 40% after agreed staging acceptance and before public launch/handover. Define acceptance criteria in writing. Target 10 working days after all inputs arrive. Delayed inputs move the schedule. Never promise technical implementation before confirming capability.

Offer a short free discovery conversation and a 3-point audit, not free production. If a prospect needs a lower fee, reduce deliverables rather than silently preserving the full scope.

Social total: nine paid cycles × ₹15,000 = ₹1,35,000. Website collections: two completed projects × ₹25,000 plus one ₹15,000 advance = ₹65,000. Total collected: ₹2,00,000.

At day 90 this model has an unfinished website and a ₹10,000 balance due later. A cleaner target is to complete Website 3 by day 90 and collect its full ₹25,000: total **₹2,10,000**, with no website balance carried over. This is the preferred operating target. Do not book extra work just to collect deposits you cannot fulfil.

Suggested social start dates: A day 15, B day 25, C day 40, D day 70. Renewal dates: A days 45/75, B days 55/85, C day 70. D starts only if capacity is demonstrated. Late-cycle payments include work continuing beyond day 90; reserve that capacity.

Preferred website schedule: Website 1 days 12–26; Website 2 days 40–54; Website 3 days 68–82. Schedule collection reminders around actual milestones, never assumed acceptance.

## 3. Who to approach

Start with coaching centres, tutors and small training institutes where the KTS background helps the conversation. Also approach local service businesses with an identifiable owner, a viable offer and an obvious website/content gap. Pick one main segment for the first 30 days.

Build the first prospect list from legitimate public business contact details and personal introductions. Prioritise active businesses that can afford the service, supply content and respond to enquiries. Do not mass scrape personal numbers or keep messaging people who decline.

## 5. Daily and weekly rhythm

Early phase, daily: 60 minutes researching and approaching 5 relevant businesses; 30 minutes on follow-ups; 45 minutes on discovery/proposals; 2–3 hours on demos or delivery; 15 minutes updating the tracker.

Weekly targets: 25–30 new personalised approaches, 15 relevant follow-ups, 2 qualified discovery calls and 1–2 scoped proposals. Across 12 active sales weeks, that gives roughly 300–360 approaches and 24 discoveries. Winning four social engagements and three websites is an ambitious hypothesis, not an established conversion rate; some clients may buy both.

Once delivery grows, protect at least 45 minutes daily for sales and follow-ups. Batch client production by account. Keep one day each week free of scheduled client work.

Follow up once after 3–4 working days and once about a week later if relevant. Then close the loop unless invited to continue. Stop immediately if declined.

## 6. Sales conversation and messages

Opening: “Hi [name], I’m [your name] from KTS Digital Marketing. I noticed [specific, accurate observation] about [business]. I have a couple of ideas for making [your course/service] clearer online. Would a short conversation be useful?”

Discovery: ask what they sell, who buys, how enquiries arrive, who follows them up, what content/assets exist, what monthly budget is available and who approves work. Discuss a useful business objective without promising leads or rankings.

Close: “Based on what you shared, I recommend [offer] at ₹[fee], covering [specific scope]. Here is the timeline and what I need from you. If the scope works for you, I’ll send the agreement and invoice.”

Send proposals within one working day of a qualified call. Each includes scope, exclusions, input dependencies, timeline, payment terms, revision limits and ownership/handover terms. No unsolicited messages are sent by the assistant; the founder authorises and sends outreach.

## 7. Delivery capacity and division of work

Initial estimates to measure: 12–16 founder hours per social client per cycle; 20–25 hours per website. At four retainers and one website, allow 68–89 delivery hours per month, 35–40 for sales, 10–15 for administration and a buffer. This is tight at five hours a day; actual time decides capacity.

Founder: prospect relationships, discovery, commercial commitments, asset collection, review, client approval, account actions, invoices and quality ownership.

Assistant, during requested work sessions: prospect qualification templates, audit preparation, proposals, calendars, copy, website implementation, checklists and reports from supplied data. The assistant is not a continuously operating employee and does not independently perform daily outreach, monitor accounts or guarantee output quality. Review all deliverables before client use.

If a social account takes more than 16 hours or a website more than 25, revise scope and estimates before selling another engagement. Never overlap two website builds initially. Add D only with at least 16 hours of spare monthly production capacity plus communication time.

## 8. Review rules when behind target

- Day 14: fewer than 3 genuine conversations? Improve prospect relevance and the opening message; ask for warm introductions.
- Day 30: below ₹55,000? Identify whether the issue is conversations, proposals, closes or collections. Prioritise the weakest stage rather than adding more services.
- Day 45: proposals repeatedly rejected? Record reasons, narrow the segment or reduce scope. Do not assume price alone is the problem.
- Day 60: below ₹1,25,000? Reforecast the remaining gap using realistic delivery capacity and signed payment milestones. Do not assume overdue balances will arrive.
- A retainer cancels? Remove future renewals from the forecast immediately. Seek a replacement through the existing pipeline; report any resulting shortfall honestly.
- Work exceeds capacity? Pause new start dates and protect current commitments. Subcontract only after agreeing the cost, access and responsibility with the founder and client as appropriate.

## 9. Track every Friday

Prospects contacted; replies; qualified calls; proposals sent; contracts won; invoices raised; cash received; overdue amounts; work delivered; hours used; direct expenses; next action per client.

Keep agency collections separate from personal money, taxes and client media budgets. This target is revenue, not take-home income. No paid ads are required by this plan. Any software purchase or specialist cost needs a budget decision; existing tools are not assumed to be free.

Choose the first segment, write down 10 businesses you know or can reach legitimately, and prepare the two offer descriptions above. Make the first five personalised approaches before polishing more website sections. The first working milestone is a qualified discovery conversation—not a follower count.
